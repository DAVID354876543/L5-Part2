Mountains

Mountains are large landforms that rise above the surrounding land in a limited area, usually in the form of a peak. They are often characterized by their steep slopes and high elevation. They play a crucial role in the Earth's climate and weather patterns. They are formed through tectonic forces or volcanism. These forces can locally raise the surface of the earth. Mountains erode slowly through the action of rivers, weather conditions, and glaciers.

Mountain ranges are formed by the movement of tectonic plates. When two plates collide, the land is pushed upwards, creating mountains. Over time, these mountains can be worn down by erosion, but new mountains can also form as tectonic activity continues. Mountains are home to diverse ecosystems and provide habitats for many species of plants and animals. They also offer recreational opportunities such as hiking, skiing, and mountaineering. The highest mountain in the world is Mount Everest, which stands at 8,848 meters (29,029 feet) above sea level. Mountains have cultural and spiritual significance in many societies, often being considered sacred or symbolic of strength and endurance. They also influence weather patterns and can create microclimates in their vicinity.
